# QueryForge Research Starter Kit

## A responsible public-information research field guide

QueryForge helps you turn a clear question into transparent, reviewable search queries for public information. It does not fetch results automatically. You inspect each query and decide whether to open a search.

## 1. Frame the research brief

Write one sentence that names the decision, audience, and evidence you need.

> Example: Find current public reports that explain how independent retailers are using AI-assisted customer support.

## 2. Choose trusted source boundaries

Use trusted organizational or institutional domains when the question benefits from primary sources.

| Goal | Query pattern |
|---|---|
| Find an exact phrase | `"your phrase"` |
| Prioritize a source | `site:example.org` |
| Find public reports | `filetype:pdf report` |
| Compare alternatives | `"topic" OR "related topic"` |
| Remove noise | `-jobs -webinar` |

## 3. Use four research lanes

| Lane | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Explore | What is publicly known about the topic? |
| Verify | Which claims have supportable evidence? |
| Find sources | Which primary reports, datasets, or publications matter? |
| Track updates | What changed recently? |

## 4. Keep the research deliberate

Do not use public search operators to seek credentials, private files, exposed databases, restricted systems, or access-control bypasses. Keep adult-content research disabled unless it is genuinely necessary for a lawful, age-appropriate research purpose.
