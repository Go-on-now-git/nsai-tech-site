AI Is Now Picking Drug Doses in Live Trials. Weight Loss Is Next.

September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

This week, an AI did something remarkable inside a live human trial: it picked the drug doses. Quotient Sciences reported that its AI system selected modified-release tablet formulations and doses during an active clinical study — and hit the study's preset pharmacokinetic target within three dosing periods. Formulation work that conventionally takes months or years of iterative lab testing and human trial-and-error, done in three tries. A safety committee still approved every composition before dosing — human oversight stayed in the loop.

Zoom out and the pattern is unmistakable. Insilico Medicine just dosed the first patient in a Phase III trial of rentosertib, a drug candidate discovered and designed by generative AI. Medicine is moving away from one-size-fits-all dosing — the same pill, the same milligrams, for everybody — toward treatments tuned like hyperparameters: optimized per patient, per biomarker, per response.

The consumer version of this shift is already here, and it's called the GLP-1 wave. Modern medical weight-loss programs don't hand you a pamphlet and wish you luck — they run clinical intake, prescribe real medication, and titrate treatment the way an engineer tunes a system. It's the difference between willpower and infrastructure.

Systems beat motivation — that's been this blog's thesis all along. Google re-engineered the cache instead of asking its models to try harder. The new medicine re-engineers the metabolism instead of asking you to try harder.

The algorithm doesn't care about your excuses. It just tunes the dose until the target hits. Maybe it's time your biology got the same treatment.
